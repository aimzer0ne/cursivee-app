How to write your name in cursive

Type it once, copy it in any of 21 styles — and pick the one that suits the letters your name actually has.

The quickest way to get your name in cursive is to type it into the cursive generator: every style updates as you type, and one tap copies the result. This page covers the part a generator cannot do for you — choosing the style that suits your name, and knowing where it will survive being pasted.

Open the cursive generator

Three steps

  1. Type your name into the box at the top of the cursive text generator. Start with a capital — cursive capitals carry most of the flourish.
  2. Compare the styles. Script, bold script and Old English read most clearly as lettering. Type “script” into the filter to see only the cursive ones.
  3. Copy and paste the one you like into your profile, caption or message.

Pick a style by the letters in your name

The light Script style has a quirk. Eleven of its letters were added to Unicode years before the other forty-one, and some devices draw them with a different font. They are the capitals B, E, F, H, I, L, M and R, and the lowercase e, g and o. That covers a lot of names:

ℰ𝓂𝓂𝒶 · ℋ𝒶𝓇𝓅ℯ𝓇 · ℒℴℊ𝒶𝓃

𝓔𝓶𝓶𝓪 · 𝓗𝓪𝓻𝓹𝓮𝓻 · 𝓛𝓸𝓰𝓪𝓷

On most iPhones the difference is slight. On some Android phones and Windows apps, the older letters come out rounder or more upright than the rest of the word. If your name starts with one of those capitals, or contains an e, g or o, bold script is the safer choice: all 52 of its letters were added together, so they always match. Capitals, initials and monograms

  • Capitalise the first letter only. A whole name in script capitals — ℰℳℳ𝒜 — is hard to read. ℰ𝓂𝓂𝒶 is not.
  • Initials work well on their own. Two or three capitals with full stops between them make a neat monogram: 𝓐. 𝓙. 𝓜.
  • One style per name. An Old English initial in front of a bold script surname can look striking in a display name, but mixing styles usually looks busier than it does in your head.
  • Use the ornament buttons in the generator to frame a name with ✧ or ♡, rather than hunting for symbols by hand.

Where a cursive name will paste, and where it won't

Most apps separate the name people see from the handle that identifies you. Cursive works in the first and almost never in the second.

Two more things before you commit. Instagram and TikTok limit how often you can change your name, so try a style in your bio first, where edits are unlimited. And search matches characters: if your display name is entirely cursive, someone typing your name in ordinary letters may not find you. Keeping your surname plain, or repeating your name in plain letters in your bio, solves that. The Instagram bio guide covers each platform in more detail.

A cursive name is not a signature

Cursive text is a sequence of characters drawn by whatever font the reader's device has. It is not an image of your handwriting, and it adds nothing to a document that a typed name would not. For a contract, sign by hand or use an e-signature tool. For a tattoo, a wedding invitation or anything printed, take your name to a real cursive typeface — there is a shortlist in cursive fonts for Google Docs, Word and Canva — so it looks the same on paper as it did on screen.

Accents and non-Latin names

Unicode's cursive alphabets cover the 26 unaccented Latin letters and nothing else. In Zoë, José or Søren, the accented letter stays plain while the rest turns cursive — 𝓩𝓸ë — because there is no script ë for any generator to use. Names in Cyrillic, Greek, Arabic, Devanagari and other scripts pass through unchanged. Dropping the accent is the only workaround, and only you can decide whether that is still your name.

Keep it readable for everyone

Screen readers do not read ℰ𝓂𝓂𝒶 as “Emma”. Some announce each letter as a mathematical symbol, others skip the characters entirely, so someone using one may hear nothing where your name should be. If your profile has a separate plain-text field, put your real name there. More in can screen readers read fancy text?
