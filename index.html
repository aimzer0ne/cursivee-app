How to use the cursive generator

  1. Type your text. Enter a name, a word or a whole caption in the box at the top. All 21 cursive fonts update as you type.

There is nothing to download and no account to make. The cursive text generator runs entirely in your browser, on any phone, tablet or computer, and keeps working offline once the page has loaded.

What is a cursive text generator?

A cursive text generator converts ordinary letters into Unicode script characters — 𝒜, 𝓑, 𝒸 and the rest — that look handwritten. Because they are characters rather than a font, they keep their shape when you copy and paste them into apps that have no font menu.

A font lives inside the app you are using, so it disappears the moment your words leave it. Script letters are different: Unicode gave them their own permanent codes, originally for mathematicians who needed a script 𝒜 that means something other than a plain A. The style is part of the text, so a cursive bio still looks cursive on someone else's phone.

That is why the same tool goes by several names — cursive generator, cursive font generator, cursive letters generator, fancy text generator. They all do one job: swap each letter you type for its cursive counterpart, so you can paste it where fonts are not allowed.

Which cursive font should you use?

All six cursive styles paste the same way, but they do not read the same way. A quick guide to the ones people use most:

Script — the classic cursive

𝒮𝒸𝓇𝒾𝓅𝓉 𝓁ℯ𝓉𝓉ℯ𝓇𝒾𝓃ℊ

Thin, looping strokes that read as formal handwriting: the style most people picture when they hear the word cursive. Its one quirk is that eleven letters — B, E, F, H, I, L, M, R, e, g and o — were encoded years before the rest, so on some devices they come out a touch different from their neighbours. The cursive alphabet guide explains why.

Bold script — the safest choice

𝓑𝓸𝓵𝓭 𝓼𝓬𝓻𝓲𝓹𝓽 𝓵𝓮𝓽𝓽𝓮𝓻𝓲𝓷𝓰

The same calligraphic shapes with heavier strokes. All 52 letters were added to Unicode together, so they always match, and the thicker lines hold up on a small phone screen where the light script can look faint. If you only try one cursive font, try this one.

Italic and bold italic — a subtle slant

𝐼𝑡𝑎𝑙𝑖𝑐 𝑙𝑒𝑡𝑡𝑒𝑟𝑖𝑛𝑔

Serif letters leaning to the right, in a light and a bold weight. They are less decorative than script, which makes them better for emphasis inside a sentence — a book title, a single stressed word — where full cursive would be too much.

Sans italic — clean and modern

𝘚𝘢𝘯𝘴 𝘪𝘵𝘢𝘭𝘪𝘤 𝘭𝘦𝘵𝘵𝘦𝘳𝘪𝘯𝘨

Slanted letters without serifs, close to the system font most apps already use. Choose it when you want a hint of style that still feels native to the app.

Old English — gothic blackletter

𝔒𝔩𝔡 𝔈𝔫𝔤𝔩𝔦𝔰𝔥 𝔩𝔢𝔱𝔱𝔢𝔯𝔦𝔫𝔤

Not cursive in the handwriting sense, but the other lettering people come looking for: medieval blackletter, popular for gaming names, band names and tattoo-style headers. Bold gothic is its heavier, easier-to-read cousin.

Bubbles, squares and the rest

These are for decoration rather than writing. Use them for a single word or a set of initials; whole sentences in ⓑⓤⓑⓑⓛⓔⓢ get hard to read fast.

Where you can paste cursive text

Cursive text works almost anywhere that accepts plain text. The rule that holds on every platform: free-text fields take it, and usernames do not. Platforms do change their rules, so paste a short test before you rely on one.

Instagram

Your bio, captions, comments and the Name field all keep cursive exactly as pasted. Your @username does not: it only accepts letters, numbers, full stops and underscores. The guide to cursive text in an Instagram bio goes through each field.

TikTok

Your bio, captions, comments and nickname take cursive. Your @username is limited to letters, numbers, full stops and underscores, the same as Instagram.

Discord

Messages, your About Me, display name and server nicknames usually accept cursive letters. Usernames are restricted to lowercase letters, numbers, full stops and underscores. For simple emphasis in a message, Discord's own formatting — *italic* and **bold** — is easier on screen readers than cursive characters.

X, Facebook, YouTube and messaging apps

Posts, bios, display names and comments accept cursive, and so do WhatsApp, iMessage, Telegram and most other chat apps. Handles on X are letters, numbers and underscores only.

What to keep plain

Anything that has to be searched for or typed back: your real name, email addresses, links, hashtags and prices. Search matches characters, so a hashtag written in script letters is a different hashtag that nobody else uses.

How the cursive generator works

Unicode's Mathematical Alphanumeric Symbols block, added in version 3.1 in 2001, holds complete alphabets in script, bold script, blackletter, double-struck, bold, italic, sans-serif and monospace. Every letter has a fixed number — script capital 𝒜 is U+1D49C — so the generator looks up each letter you type and swaps it for its partner in the style you chose. Bubbles, squares and wide letters come from other blocks, and the strikethrough, underline and slashed effects add a combining mark after each letter.

A few script and blackletter letters existed in Unicode before that block did, so it has holes, and the generator fills them from the older Letterlike Symbols range — that is where ℬ, ℋ and ℯ come from. Tips and limits

  • Keep the important words plain. Screen readers read script letters as maths symbol names or skip them entirely. Use cursive for flair, and keep your name and anything that matters in ordinary letters. More in fancy text and screen readers.
  • The letters do not join up. Each cursive letter is a separate character, so words look like elegant print rather than connected handwriting. For true joined-up script, you need a real font.
  • Numbers stay plain in script styles. Unicode has no script digits, and a few alphabets have gaps. Characters without a cursive form pass through unchanged — no generator can invent them.
  • It can count double. Some apps count each script letter as two characters, so a bio near its limit may suddenly be too long. Style one phrase instead of the whole thing.
  • Shapes vary by device. Each phone draws these characters with its own fonts. If you see boxes or question marks, read why fancy text shows up as boxes.
  • Latin letters only. Unicode has no script alphabet for Cyrillic or other non-Latin writing, and no script forms of accented letters such as é or ñ, so those pass through unchanged.
  • Private and offline. Every conversion runs in your browser. Nothing you type is sent or stored, and the page keeps working without a connection once it has loaded.

How do I get a cursive font on Instagram, TikTok or Discord?

Is this a cursive font generator?

Can I use cursive text in Google Docs or Word?

You can paste it and it will display, but a real cursive font is the better choice in a document: it joins letters smoothly, prints properly, can be spell-checked, and screen readers can read it. The guide to cursive fonts in Google Docs, Word and Canva lists good ones and where to find them.

Can I make a cursive signature with this?

Only the look of one. The generator gives you cursive text characters, not a handwritten signature image. For signing a document you still need a drawn signature or an e-signature tool.

Does it work on iPhone and Android?

Yes. It runs in any modern browser on iPhone, Android, Mac, Windows or Chromebook, with nothing to install, and keeps working offline once loaded. The exact letter shapes depend on the fonts of the device displaying them, so they differ slightly between phones.

Why did some of my letters or numbers not change?

Because that character does not exist in Unicode. These alphabets were added for mathematicians and typesetters, and a few have gaps — the script styles have no digits, for example. Anything without a cursive form passes through unchanged.

Why does my cursive text show up as boxes or question marks?

The device showing it has no font for those characters, or the app has replaced them. The sender cannot fix it from their side. The guide to fancy text showing as boxes explains each symptom and which styles are safest.

Why does cursive text use up more characters?

Script letters sit outside the part of Unicode most apps were built around, and some character counters treat each one as two. If a bio or post suddenly looks too long, put one phrase in cursive instead of the whole thing.
