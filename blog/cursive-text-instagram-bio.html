How to put cursive text in your Instagram bio

There is no font menu on Instagram, TikTok or Discord. Here is how cursive bios get there anyway, which fields accept them, and the three places they reliably fail.

The short answer

  1. Open the cursive text generator and type the line you want.
  2. Pick a style — Script and Bold script are the two that read as handwriting — and press Copy.
  3. In Instagram, go to Edit profile, tap the Bio field, paste, and save.

That is the whole process, and it is the same on every platform: copy, paste into a field that takes text, save. The rest of this page is about why it works, and about the fields where it does not.

Why there is no font setting to find

People go looking for a font option in Instagram's settings and there isn't one. The app picks the typeface, and every profile gets the same one. What a generator gives you is not a font at all but a different set of characters. Unicode, the standard that numbers every character your phone can show, includes a complete script alphabet that was added for mathematicians: 𝒜 is a separate character from A in the same way that B is.

Because the difference is in the text rather than in a style applied to it, it survives being copied. Instagram is not being asked to change its typeface. It is being handed characters that already look like that. The about page goes into more of the background.

Where it works, platform by platform

The pattern is consistent enough to memorise: free-text fields accept these characters, and handles do not. This reflects each platform's published rules at the time of writing; they do change, so paste a short test before you rely on one.

Captions, comments, posts and direct messages behave like bios: they are free text, so the characters go through. Discord messages also have real formatting built in — wrapping a word in asterisks makes it italic or bold — and where a platform offers that, it is the better choice, for reasons covered under accessibility below.

Three things that catch people out

Each fancy letter can count as two

Instagram bios stop at 150 characters and X posts at 280. Script letters sit in a part of Unicode that some character counters treat as two units each, so a line that fits comfortably in plain letters can be rejected as too long in cursive. If a field refuses text that looks short enough, this is almost always why. Style one phrase rather than the whole bio and the problem goes away.

Search may stop finding you

Search matches characters. Some platforms quietly fold fancy letters back to plain ones when they index a profile; plenty do not. If your display name is written entirely in script, someone typing your name in ordinary letters may not find you. The same goes for hashtags and keywords: a hashtag in script letters is, at best, a different hashtag that nobody else uses. Keep anything you want to be found by in plain text.

Names can only be changed so often

Instagram limits how often you can edit the Name field, and TikTok does the same for nicknames. Test a style in your bio first, where edits are unlimited, and look at it on a second device before you spend a name change on it.

Making it look good rather than loud

  • One line, not five. Cursive works as an accent. A whole bio in script is slower to read and loses the contrast that made it interesting.
  • Prefer bold script at small sizes. The lighter script has thin strokes that fade on a phone screen. Bold script holds up better and is drawn more consistently across devices. There is a letter-by-letter comparison in the cursive alphabet reference.
  • Leave contact details alone. Emails, links, prices and dates should stay in plain characters so they can be tapped, copied and read aloud.

If it does not look right after pasting

  • Empty boxes or question marks mean the device has no font for those characters. See why fancy text shows up as boxes.
  • Plain letters came back means the platform normalised your text on save. Nothing can be done in that field.
  • A few letters stayed plain means that style has no character for them. Numbers, punctuation and accented letters have no script forms at all.

One caveat worth taking seriously

Screen readers do not read 𝒽ℯ𝓁𝓁ℴ as a word. They announce a list of mathematical symbol names or skip it entirely, so anyone using one cannot tell what your bio says. Use cursive for decoration and keep the information in ordinary letters. What screen readers do with fancy text covers this properly.
