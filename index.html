Common questions

Is this actually a font?

No — and that is exactly why it works. A font is a set of drawings your app chooses. These are Unicode characters: 𝒜 and A are as different as A and B, with their own permanent numbers in the standard. Because the difference lives in the text rather than in a style setting, it survives the trip into apps that never offered you a typeface menu.

Will it work on Instagram, TikTok or Discord?

In bios and captions, almost always. In usernames, it varies — some platforms strip these characters, some replace them with plain letters, and some reject the entry outright. Paste a short test before you commit to anything you would hate to lose.

Why did some of my letters not change?

Because that character does not exist. Unicode added these alphabets for mathematicians and typesetters, one at a time, so a few have gaps. Any letter without a counterpart comes through untouched, and no generator anywhere can invent it.

Is it free, and does it keep what I type?

Free, with no account and no limit. Every conversion runs in your own browser — nothing you type is sent anywhere, logged, or stored. Close the tab and it is gone. The privacy page spells this out.

The long way round

You might be surprised to know that a long time ago, a group of people in charge of making rules for writing math stuff got together to discuss something pretty specific. They were trying to figure out if mathematicians really needed a special letter ℬ that would mean something different from a regular bold B. And guess what? They decided that yes, they did need it. This decision might seem like a small thing, but it's actually the reason why you can make your captions look like they're handwritten today. It's pretty cool to think that a choice made for some complicated math equations that probably only physics professors would understand ended up having a bigger impact.

You know what's funny about this page? Every style of writing here is actually just a coincidence from the past. The blackletter style, for example, was only kept around because of medieval scholarship. And have you ever wondered why we use fullwidth letters? It's because they were used on old Japanese typewriters. Even the circled characters came from signs you see when you're traveling. None of these styles were meant to be used on social media, but somehow they all work really well together anyway. 🧵

There's something really odd about this. Most of the time, when you're online, you're asked to pick a font, which is basically like asking for permission from the app you're using. But this is different. It doesn't ask for anything. The letters are the message, and that's it. You can copy and paste them into any field, even if it doesn't know anything about typography, and they'll still show up just fine. That's because there's nothing extra to remove - just the characters, doing their thing. ✨

The thing is, there's a problem with using fancy fonts and symbols in your writing. 🤔 When a screen reader comes across something like 𝓱𝓮𝓵𝓵𝓸, it doesn't see a word, it sees a bunch of mathematical symbols all lined up. 🔍 And then it either reads them out one by one, which can be really confusing, or it just skips over them altogether and says nothing. That's a real person who's having a harder time because of your choice, just so your bio can look fancy. 📄 It's okay to use these kinds of things to add some flair or decoration, or for a name that's already easy to read. But for the important parts, it's better to stick with regular letters. That way, everyone can understand what you're trying to say, no matter how they're reading it. 📖

So what's next? Just keep going, I guess. 🖋️ Start writing, go through all twenty-one alphabets, and see which two you like best - mark them with a star or something. Then, just take those two with you. The best part is, it's free, it doesn't take up any space, and you can use it even when you're not online, which is pretty rare for things on the internet these days.
