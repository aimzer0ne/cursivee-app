Common questions

Why do some letters come through unchanged?

Because no character in that script resembles them closely enough to be worth faking. Rather than substitute something from an unrelated alphabet — which is how text ends up as boxes or with its word order scrambled — the letter is left alone. Q and V are the usual holdouts.

Is lookalike text safe to use?

For a caption, yes. For anything that identifies you, no. Mixing Greek, Cyrillic and Latin letters is the exact technique behind spoofed domains and impersonated usernames, so platforms block it and security tools flag it. Fun in a post; a bad idea in a handle.

Why does morse drop some of my characters?

Because International Morse only defines letters, digits and a short list of punctuation. Anything outside that set is dropped rather than guessed at — a wrong signal would be worse than a missing one.

Will a blind person be able to read the braille output?

Not as fluent braille. These are Unicode braille patterns — the dot shapes themselves — not a contracted braille translation, and they are not connected to a refreshable display. Treat it as a visual effect, not an accessibility feature.

The long way round

🙃 A page built entirely on coincidence

卂 isn't an A. 🌍 It's a Chinese character with its own meaning, its own pronunciation, and a few thousand years of history that have nothing whatsoever to do with our alphabet. It just happens to look a bit like one. That's the only reason it ever ended up in your caption.

Almost everything here works that way. ᚱ is a real Elder Futhark rune. Ꮢ comes from the Cherokee syllabary, which Sequoyah invented in the 1820s. ₮ is a currency symbol. None of them were designed to stand in for our letters — they got picked, centuries later, by people who spotted a resemblance and had a use for it. 🎲

Then there's the other half of the page: morse, braille, binary. 📻 Those aren't decoration at all. They're real encodings, each one built so a message could survive something that couldn't carry letters — a telegraph wire, a fingertip, a switch that's either on or off. Putting them next to vaporwave is a bit silly, but online they get used for the same reason: to make a sentence take the scenic route.

One thing here does deserve care. ⚠️ The lookalike letters — the Greek and Cyrillic ones that pass for Latin — are exactly what people use to fake domain names and copy usernames. Lovely in a caption. In a handle it's the move that gets accounts suspended, and the systems watching for it don't much care that you meant it playfully.

The rest is harmless. 🎈 Mirror a sentence, put it in mocking caps, rebuild it out of runes, send it as dots and dashes. Nothing on this page has ever had to justify itself, which is honestly the best thing about it.
