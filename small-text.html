What this small text generator does

This is a small text generator — the thing people also search for as a tiny text generator or a small caps generator. It does not shrink a font. It swaps each letter for a genuinely smaller Unicode character, which is why the result survives a copy and paste into a bio that has no font settings at all. Superscript, subscript, small capitals

The same box covers what gets searched as a superscript generator, a subscript generator, a tiny caps generator or a small letters generator. Raised text sits at the top of the line, lowered text drops beneath it, and small capitals stay on the baseline at roughly x-height. People also ask for a small text converter or text to small text, which is the same job under a different name.

What it is not

It will not save you characters. A small font generator sounds like it should shorten your text, but every small letter still counts as one character and some count as more, so a bio already at its limit gains nothing. It is also not a font size changer — nothing here changes the size setting of the app you paste into, only which characters you paste. And because Unicode never finished the set, a few letters have no small form at all and come through at normal size.

Common questions

What is the difference between small caps and superscript?

Small caps sit on the baseline at roughly x-height — ʟɪᴋᴇ ᴛʜɪꜱ — so they read as quiet capitals. Superscript lifts the letters up to the top of the line, ˡⁱᵏᵉ ᵗʰⁱˢ, which reads as a footnote or an aside. Small caps stay legible at length; superscript is best in short bursts.

Can I use small text in a username?

Sometimes. These are modifier and phonetic characters rather than plain letters, and username validators are the strictest fields on any platform. Bios and display names are a far safer bet. Test before you commit.

Why does subscript have so many gaps?

Subscript letters were added for chemistry and mathematics — the ones needed for notation got encoded, the rest never did. That leaves no subscript b, c, d, f, g, q, w, y or z anywhere in Unicode, so those letters stay full size.

Does small text actually take up less space?

Visually, yes. In character count, no — each small letter still counts as one character, and a few count as more. A bio at its limit will not gain room by shrinking.

The long way round

🔎 Tiny letters, borrowed from a chemistry lab

Nobody sat down to invent cute small text. 🧪 These letters exist because a chemist needed to write H₂O, a linguist needed a raised ʰ, and a mathematician needed to label a variable. Somewhere along the way people started putting them in their bios instead.

Which explains the gaps — and the gaps are really the whole story of this page. There's no subscript b. No subscript g. No superscript q. Nobody needed them, so nobody made them, and thirty years later one letter in your username simply refuses to shrink. 🤷

That isn't a bug here, or anywhere else. It's a committee's to-do list that stopped halfway, and you've wandered into the part they never got round to finishing.

Plenty of tools hide this. They'll swap the missing letter for something from an unrelated alphabet and hope you don't notice, which works fine right up until a platform flags your name or someone's phone shows a box. 📦 We'd rather just show you the gap. Pick a different word — that one will always render.

What small text is genuinely good at is volume. 🔉 Normal letters shout. These ones don't. Your pronouns, your city, your job title, anything you want present but not competing — drop it a size and the bio more or less sorts itself out.

Just remember what you're trading. ⚖️ A screen reader doesn't hear a whisper, it hears phonetic symbols. Keep the important parts full size and save these for the places where tone matters more than the words do. The whole set lives at https://cursivee.app, free and without an account.
