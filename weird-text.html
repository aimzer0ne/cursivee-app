How to use the weird text generator

  1. Type your text. Enter it in the box at the top of the page. All fifteen styles update as you type.
  2. Find a style. Scroll the list, or filter it by name — try “morse” or “mirrored”.
  3. Copy and paste. Press Copy and paste the result into a bio, caption, message or post.

What is weird text?

Weird text swaps your letters for characters from other writing systems that happen to look like ours — a Chinese 卂 for A, a rune ᚱ for R, a currency sign ₮ for T. Because they are real Unicode characters, not a font, they paste anywhere plain text is accepted.

None of these characters were made to stand in for Latin letters. ᚱ is a real Elder Futhark rune; Ꮢ comes from the Cherokee syllabary Sequoyah created in the 1820s. The page also rearranges your own letters (mirrored, mocking caps, vaporwave) and encodes them (morse, braille, binary).

Where weird text works

  • Works everywhere: the novelty styles. Mocking caps and spaced-out text are still ordinary letters.
  • Usually works: the strange alphabets in captions, bios, comments and chats on Instagram, TikTok, Discord and X. How they look depends on the reader's fonts.
  • Avoid: lookalike letters in usernames and handles. Mixing scripts is the trick behind spoofed accounts, so platforms block it.

For decorative rather than strange lettering, try the cursive text generator. To damage your text instead, the glitch and cursed generators are next door.

Tips and limits

  • It is not a translator. It borrows shapes, not meaning — a Cherokee reader will not read your sentence as Cherokee.
  • Codes run one way. Morse and binary are written, not decoded. Morse drops any character it has no signal for rather than guessing.
  • Braille is visual only. The output is Unicode braille patterns, not a contracted braille translation, so treat it as an effect rather than an accessibility feature.
  • Some letters stay unchanged. If no character looks close enough, the letter is left alone instead of faked — Q and V are the usual holdouts.

Weird text generator FAQ

How do I make weird text?

Type in the box at the top of the page. Your words are rebuilt underneath in fifteen weird styles at once, from runes to binary. Press Copy on the one you like and paste it anywhere plain text is accepted.

Is weird text a font?

No. Every style is made of real Unicode characters borrowed from other scripts and symbol sets, or of your own letters rearranged. That is why it pastes into apps with no font settings, and why it looks slightly different from one device to the next.

Why do some letters come through unchanged?

Because no character in that script looks close enough to be worth using. Rather than substitute something from an unrelated alphabet — which leads to boxes or scrambled text — the letter is left alone. Q and V are the usual holdouts.

Is lookalike text safe to use?

In a caption, yes. In anything that identifies you, no. Mixing Greek, Cyrillic and Latin letters is the technique behind spoofed web addresses and impersonated usernames, so platforms block it and security tools flag it.

Can I convert text to morse code or binary here?

Yes, in one direction. The Morse style writes your text as dots and dashes with a slash between words, and the Binary style writes each letter as eight bits. The page does not decode morse or binary back into letters.

Why does morse drop some of my characters?

International Morse only defines letters, digits and a short list of punctuation. Anything outside that set is dropped rather than guessed at, because a wrong signal is worse than a missing one.

Can a blind person read the braille output?

Not as fluent braille. These are Unicode braille patterns — the dot shapes — not a contracted braille translation, and they are not sent to a braille display. Treat it as a visual effect, not an accessibility feature.

Why does my weird text show up as boxes?

The device displaying it has no font for that script. Runes and Cherokee are less widely supported than everyday letters, especially on older phones. The novelty styles never have this problem; the guide to fancy text showing as boxes explains the rest.
